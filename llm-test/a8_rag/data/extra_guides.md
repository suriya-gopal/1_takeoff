# Extra documents (used only in the data-scaling experiment)

TakeOff help pages and general travel advice. These are added to the destination guides to test how a larger,
more mixed knowledge base changes retrieval.

## TakeOff help: planning a trip

### Creating a trip

To plan a trip in TakeOff, sign in and choose Plan a trip. A trip has a title, start and end dates, a budget range in US dollars, a group size and up to several city stops in order, each with a number of nights. The form checks that the end date is after the start date and that the budget minimum is not above the maximum. A new trip starts as a private draft, so only its owner can see it. The trip page then shows the route, the itinerary items added so far and links to the budget, day planner and other tools for that trip.

### Public trips and join requests

A trip can stay private or be posted publicly. Public trips appear on the Community trips page and in search, where other members can filter by destination, dates and budget. If the owner leaves seats open, other members can send a request to join with a short message. The owner sees each request as pending and can accept or decline it; accepted members become companions on the trip. A member cannot send a second request for the same trip while one is still pending, and an owner cannot request to join their own trip.

### Verified travelers

Members who sign up with a university or company email address are marked as verified travelers. The badge appears next to their name on public trips and join requests, so trip owners can see that the person has confirmed an email address from a known domain. Verification does not check identity documents; it only confirms control of the email address. Members can also sign in with a Google account instead of a username and password. Each account has one traveler profile with a display name and a short bio of up to 200 characters.

### Day-by-day drafts

On a trip you own, Draft my days asks TakeOff's on-device language model to suggest a morning, afternoon and evening for each day at each stop. You can describe your interests in a few words and tick a box if you are traveling with kids. The draft is only a suggestion: nothing is saved until you press Save to my itinerary, which adds the items at 9 am, 2 pm and 7 pm. Small models can invent place names, so check each suggestion before booking. Each member can request a limited number of drafts per hour.

### Budget, insights and exports

The budget page converts a public trip's budget into the local currency of each stop using live exchange rates, so travelers can see what their dollars are worth on the ground. The Insights page has interactive charts of public trips by destination and departures by month. The Reports page shows community totals, and trips can be downloaded as CSV or JSON files. TakeOff's destination list includes an estimated average daily cost per person for each city, which is used to compare destinations and to set the budget band for AI drafts.

### Packing list and place suggestions

When the optional hosted assistant is switched on, two more tools appear on a trip. Find places suggests six things to do or eat in one city of the trip, each with a reason, a typical cost and tags, and a button to add it to the itinerary. The packing list builds a checklist from the route, the dates, the typical weather for that time of year and the planned activities. If the hosted assistant is not set up, these pages say so and the rest of the site works normally.

## General travel advice

### Passports and entry rules

Before booking international flights, check that your passport will be valid for long enough. Many countries in Europe's Schengen area require a passport valid for at least three months after the planned departure date, and some countries in Asia ask for six months. Entry rules change, so check the official government site of each country shortly before travel. Some countries that do not require a visa still require an online travel authorization before departure. Airlines can refuse boarding if documents are not in order.

### Travel insurance

Travel insurance can cover medical emergencies abroad, trip cancellation, delayed or lost luggage and missed connections. Home health insurance often does not cover care overseas, and medical evacuation can cost tens of thousands of dollars. Policies differ a lot in what they exclude, such as pre-existing conditions, adventure sports or cancellations for reasons not listed in the policy. Some credit cards include limited travel cover when the trip is paid with the card. Read the policy wording, not just the summary, before relying on it.

### Jet lag

Jet lag is usually worse when flying east than west. To adjust faster, switch to the local schedule as soon as you arrive: eat meals at local times, get outdoor daylight in the morning, and avoid long naps on the first afternoon. Staying hydrated on the flight and limiting alcohol and late caffeine help. A common rule of thumb is that the body adjusts by roughly one time zone per day, so on a short trip across many time zones it can make sense to plan lighter activities for the first day or two.

### Money and cards abroad

Cards are widely accepted in most of Europe, North America and Japan, but carrying a small amount of local cash helps for markets, small cafes and transport machines. Cards without foreign transaction fees save about three percent on every purchase. When a card machine abroad offers to charge in your home currency, choosing the local currency is usually cheaper, because the conversion offered at the till often uses a poor exchange rate. Tell your bank before traveling if it may block foreign transactions.

### Phones and data

Roaming on a home phone plan can be expensive, so many travelers buy a local SIM card or an eSIM data plan before leaving. eSIMs can be installed in minutes on most recent phones and activated on arrival. Offline maps and translation apps are worth downloading before the trip in case the connection drops. Free public Wi-Fi is common in cafes, hotels and stations, but it is safer to avoid entering passwords or payment details on open networks without a VPN.

### Packing light

Packing light makes trains, stairs and old city centers much easier, especially in places with cobblestones and many bridges. A carry-on suitcase or backpack is usually enough for one to two weeks if clothes can be washed on the way. Layers work better than bulky items, and a light waterproof jacket is useful almost everywhere. Keep medicines, documents, chargers and one change of clothes in the bag that stays with you, in case checked luggage is delayed. Many budget airlines charge for larger cabin bags, so check the size rules before flying.

### Rail passes in Japan

The Japan Rail Pass gives unlimited travel on most JR trains, including most shinkansen, for 7, 14 or 21 days. After a large price increase in 2023, the national pass is worth it mainly for long trips that cover many cities, such as Tokyo to Hiroshima and back. For a shorter route, separate tickets or regional passes from JR West or JR East are often cheaper. The pass does not cover private railways or subways, and the fastest Nozomi and Mizuho trains need an extra ticket.

### Trains in Italy

Italy's high-speed trains, run by Trenitalia (Frecciarossa) and Italo, connect Milan, Florence, Rome, Naples and Venice in a few hours, often faster than flying once airport time is included. Booking early gives the cheapest fares. Regional trains are slower and have fixed prices, and paper regional tickets must be validated in a machine on the platform before boarding, or travelers can be fined. Strikes are announced in advance, so check the news a few days before travel. Major stations have staffed ticket offices as well as machines and apps.

### Traveling with children

With children, plan fewer sights per day and build in breaks at parks, playgrounds and cafes. Booking family rooms or apartments with a kitchen can save money on meals. Many museums in Europe are free or discounted for children, and some have family trails or audio guides for kids. Strollers are hard to use on stairs, cobblestones and crowded transport, so a carrier can be easier for small children. Bring snacks and a small first aid kit, and check whether car seats are needed for taxis or rental cars.

### Traveling sustainably

Trains usually produce far fewer emissions than short flights, and in Europe and Japan they often take city center to city center in similar time. Staying longer in fewer places reduces transport and makes trips less rushed. Choosing locally owned guesthouses, restaurants and guides keeps more money in the community. Reusable water bottles cut plastic waste, and in many cities tap water is safe to drink. Respecting local rules at popular sites, such as staying on paths and following photo restrictions, helps limit overtourism.

### Travel safety

Most popular destinations are safe for travelers, but pickpocketing is common in crowded places such as busy trains, tourist trams and famous squares. Carry wallets and phones in front pockets or a zipped bag, and keep a copy of your passport separate from the original. Use official taxis or ride apps, and be cautious of strangers offering help at ticket machines. Save the local emergency number; in the European Union it is 112, in the United States 911, and in Japan 110 for police and 119 for fire and ambulance.

### Choosing when to travel

Shoulder seasons, the weeks just before and after the peak summer period, often offer the best balance of good weather, open attractions and lower prices. Peak holidays such as Christmas, Easter, Golden Week in Japan and school summer breaks raise prices for flights and hotels. Festivals can make a trip memorable but also fill hotels months ahead. Checking typical temperatures and rainfall for each month helps avoid surprises, such as rainy seasons or extreme heat. Booking refundable hotel rates keeps plans flexible if the forecast or prices change.

### Accessibility when traveling

Travelers with limited mobility should check access details for each stop before booking. Old city centers can have steps, steep streets and uneven paving, while newer transport systems often have lifts and step-free routes. Airlines and rail companies usually offer free assistance if it is booked in advance, often at least 48 hours before travel. Hotels may list a room as accessible even if the bathroom has a step, so it is worth asking for photos or exact measurements. Many major museums lend wheelchairs free of charge at the entrance.
