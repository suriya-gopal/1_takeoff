# TakeOff destination guides

Short guides for the twelve destinations travelers can pick in TakeOff. Each section is one topic for one city.
Prices and opening rules change, so figures are approximate and should be checked before booking.

## Chicago, United States

### Getting around

Chicago is easy to see without a car. The CTA runs the "L" trains and city buses, and riders pay with a Ventra card, the Ventra app or a contactless bank card tapped at the gate. The Blue Line runs around the clock between O'Hare International Airport and downtown, a ride of roughly 45 minutes, while the Orange Line links Midway Airport to the Loop. Divvy bikes are available across the city, and the Lakefront Trail gives cyclists and runners about 18 miles of path beside Lake Michigan. Downtown streets follow a grid, so walking between the Loop, River North and the Magnificent Mile is simple. Parking downtown is expensive, which is another reason most visitors leave the car at home.

### Sights and things to do

Start in Millennium Park, home of the mirrored Cloud Gate sculpture, then walk next door to the Art Institute of Chicago. An architecture boat tour on the Chicago River is the most popular way to understand the skyline and the city's role in the history of the skyscraper. Families tend to head for the Museum Campus, where the Field Museum, Shedd Aquarium and Adler Planetarium sit side by side, or to the Museum of Science and Industry in Hyde Park. Lincoln Park Zoo is free to enter. Navy Pier has a Ferris wheel and boat rides, and Wrigley Field hosts Cubs baseball through the summer.

### Food, seasons and costs

Chicago is known for deep-dish pizza, the Chicago-style hot dog, which is traditionally served without ketchup, and the Italian beef sandwich. Summer is the busiest season, with free concerts in Millennium Park and festivals almost every weekend; Lollapalooza fills Grant Park in early August and hotel prices jump that week. Winters are long, cold and windy, so visitors from December to March should pack a warm coat, hat and gloves. In TakeOff's destination data a typical day in Chicago costs about 170 US dollars per person, including a mid-range hotel share, meals and local transport.

## New York, United States

### Getting around

The New York subway runs 24 hours a day, every day. Riders tap a contactless bank card, phone or OMNY card at the turnstile, and OMNY caps weekly spending: after 12 paid rides in a seven-day period, the rest of that week's rides are free. Buses use the same payment. From JFK Airport, the AirTrain connects to the subway at Jamaica and Howard Beach, and to the Long Island Rail Road at Jamaica; a taxi from JFK to Manhattan has a flat fare. The free Staten Island Ferry offers views of the Statue of Liberty and the harbor. Manhattan above 14th Street follows a numbered grid, which makes walking easy.

### Sights and things to do

Central Park covers 843 acres and is best explored on foot or by rented bike. The Metropolitan Museum of Art sits on its eastern edge, and the American Museum of Natural History is on the western side. The High Line, a park built on an old elevated railway, runs through the Meatpacking District and Chelsea. Ferries to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island leave from Battery Park, and tickets that include the pedestal or crown sell out early. Walking across the Brooklyn Bridge into DUMBO is free and gives a classic skyline view.

### Shows and evenings

Broadway shows are the city's signature evening out. Full-price tickets are expensive, but the TKTS booth in Times Square sells discounted same-day tickets, and many shows run digital lotteries for cheap seats. Most theaters are dark one night a week, often Monday, so check the schedule before planning. For a cheaper night, comedy clubs in Greenwich Village and free summer events such as Shakespeare in the Park are popular. Rooftop bars in Midtown and Brooklyn are busiest at sunset in warm months.

### Food, seasons and costs

New York is famous for pizza sold by the slice, bagels, pastrami sandwiches from Jewish delis and food from almost every country, especially in Queens. Tipping of 18 to 20 percent is expected at sit-down restaurants. Spring and autumn have the most comfortable weather; summers are hot and humid, and the weeks around Thanksgiving and Christmas bring the Macy's parade, holiday windows and the highest hotel prices of the year. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in New York at about 220 US dollars per person, the highest of the American cities in the app.

## Rome, Italy

### Getting around

Rome's historic center is compact, and most visitors walk between the main sights. The Metro has three lines, A, B and C, which are handy for reaching the Vatican and the Colosseum but do not reach every part of the old center, where buses and trams fill the gaps. Single tickets are time-based, and contactless bank cards work at Metro gates. From Fiumicino Airport (FCO), the Leonardo Express train reaches Termini station in about 32 minutes; regional trains to Trastevere are slower but cheaper. Licensed taxis from Fiumicino to the old center charge a fixed fare. Driving in the center is restricted by a limited traffic zone, so renting a car is not useful.

### Ancient Rome and the Vatican

A combined ticket covers the Colosseum, the Roman Forum and the Palatine Hill, and timed entry slots for the Colosseum sell out in high season, so book ahead. The Vatican Museums, which end at the Sistine Chapel, also need an advance booking and are normally closed on Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month, when entry is free and crowds are heavy. St. Peter's Basilica is free, but shoulders and knees must be covered. The Pantheon now charges a small entry fee, and throwing a coin into the Trevi Fountain is said to guarantee a return to Rome.

### Food, seasons and costs

Roman pasta classics are carbonara, cacio e pepe and amatriciana; supplì, fried rice balls with mozzarella, make a cheap snack. Trastevere and the area around Campo de' Fiori are lively for dinner. Many restaurants add a small cover charge called coperto, and tipping is optional. Public drinking fountains, called nasoni, give free cold water all over the city. In August many locals leave on holiday around Ferragosto on 15 August, so some family restaurants close. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Rome at about 125 US dollars per person.

### Practical tips

Rome is hot in July and August, often above 32 degrees Celsius, so plan outdoor sights for the morning and museums for the afternoon. Spring and October are the most pleasant months. Pickpockets work crowded buses, especially line 64 to the Vatican, and busy sights. Hotels charge a city tax per person per night, paid at check-in. With children, the Explora children's museum and gladiator-themed tours near the Colosseum are popular, and gelato breaks help on long walking days. Strollers struggle on cobblestones, so a baby carrier is easier.

## Florence, Italy

### Getting around

Florence is small and best seen on foot; the walk from Santa Maria Novella train station to the Duomo takes about ten minutes. The center is a limited traffic zone (ZTL), and cars without a permit are fined automatically, so visitors who rent a car should park outside it. Many travelers arrive by high-speed train from Rome, which takes about 1 hour 30 minutes, or from Milan, about 1 hour 55 minutes. Florence's small airport (FLR) is linked to the center by tram line T2. Pisa, with the larger airport and the Leaning Tower, is about an hour away by train.

### Sights and museums

The Duomo's dome by Brunelleschi can be climbed, but entry is by timed reservation as part of a combined cathedral ticket. The Uffizi Gallery holds Botticelli's Birth of Venus, and Michelangelo's David stands in the Accademia Gallery; both have long queues, so booking a slot ahead saves hours. The Ponte Vecchio, lined with jewelry shops, crosses the Arno to the Oltrarno district and the Pitti Palace and Boboli Gardens. For the classic view of the city, walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo at sunset.

### Food, day trips and costs

Florentine specialties include bistecca alla fiorentina, a thick T-bone steak sold by weight and served rare, and lampredotto, a tripe sandwich from street carts. The Mercato Centrale has a food hall upstairs with many cheap options. Popular day trips go to Siena, San Gimignano and the Chianti wine region by bus, car or organized tour. Florence charges a tourist tax per night at hotels. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Florence at about 115 US dollars per person.

## Venice, Italy

### Getting around

Venice has no cars; people travel on foot or by water. Vaporetto water buses run along the Grand Canal and out to the islands, and passes for one, two, three or seven days are cheaper than buying several single tickets. The traghetto, a gondola ferry that crosses the Grand Canal at a few points, is a cheap way to ride a gondola briefly. Private gondola rides follow an official fixed rate, which is higher in the evening. Streets are narrow with many stepped bridges, so pack light; rolling suitcases are hard work. From Marco Polo Airport (VCE), the Alilaguna boat goes to the center and buses go to Piazzale Roma.

### Sights and islands

St. Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace face St. Mark's Square, and both are busiest in the late morning. The Rialto Bridge and its market are a short walk away. Murano is known for glassblowing and Burano for its brightly painted houses and lace; both are reached by vaporetto and make an easy half-day trip. On certain peak days in spring and summer, day visitors who are not staying overnight must pay an access fee booked online in advance, so check the calendar before planning a day trip.

### Food, seasons and costs

Venetians snack on cicchetti, small bites served in wine bars called bacari, usually with a glass of wine or a spritz. Local dishes include sarde in saor, sweet-and-sour sardines, and risotto made black with squid ink. From autumn to early spring, high tides called acqua alta can flood low areas such as St. Mark's Square; the MOSE flood barriers now protect the city from the worst tides, and sirens warn of high water. Winter is quiet except during Carnival in February. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Venice at about 150 US dollars per person.

## Milan, Italy

### Getting around

Milan has five Metro lines, M1 to M5, plus trams and buses, and riders can tap a contactless bank card at Metro gates. From Malpensa Airport (MXP), the Malpensa Express train reaches Cadorna or Centrale station in roughly 40 to 50 minutes, and airport buses run to Centrale. Linate Airport, much closer to the city, is linked by Metro line M4. Milano Centrale is a major rail hub, with high-speed trains to Venice, Florence and Rome and regional trains to the lakes.

### Sights and day trips

The Duomo, Milan's Gothic cathedral, has rooftop terraces reached by stairs or lift, with views of the spires. Next to it is the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, a glass-roofed shopping arcade. Leonardo da Vinci's Last Supper is in the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie, and visits are short timed slots in small groups that often sell out weeks in advance, so book as early as possible. La Scala is one of the world's best known opera houses. Lake Como is a popular day trip: trains from Milan reach Varenna in about an hour, and ferries cross to Bellagio.

### Food, evenings and costs

Milanese dishes include saffron risotto alla milanese, cotoletta, a breaded veal cutlet, and ossobuco. Panettone, the sweet Christmas bread, comes from Milan. The early-evening aperitivo is a local habit: from about 6 to 9 pm many bars serve snacks or a small buffet with a drink, and the Navigli canal district is the most popular area for it. Milan is busiest and most expensive during its fashion weeks and the Salone del Mobile design fair in April. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Milan at about 140 US dollars per person.

## Tokyo, Japan

### Getting around

Tokyo's trains are fast and punctual. A rechargeable IC card such as Suica or Pasmo, or the mobile version on a phone, works on nearly all trains, subways and buses and in many shops. The JR Yamanote Line loops around central Tokyo and links Shinjuku, Shibuya, Harajuku, Tokyo Station and Ueno. Most lines stop running between about midnight and 5 am, so late nights may end with a taxi. Haneda Airport (HND) is much closer to the center than Narita, about 30 minutes by train or monorail. Rush hour, roughly 8 to 9:30 am on weekdays, is extremely crowded and best avoided with luggage.

### Neighborhoods and sights

Shibuya is known for its scramble crossing, and Shinjuku for its skyscrapers, the tiny bars of Golden Gai and the free observation decks of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building. Asakusa has Senso-ji, Tokyo's oldest temple, and the Nakamise shopping street. Harajuku sits next to the forested Meiji Shrine. Akihabara is the center for electronics, anime and games, and Ueno Park holds several major museums and a zoo. For families, teamLab's digital art museums and the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka are favorites; Ghibli tickets are sold in advance only.

### Food

Tokyo has more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city, but some of the best meals are cheap: ramen, soba, tempura and conveyor-belt sushi. Convenience stores such as 7-Eleven, Lawson and FamilyMart sell good onigiri, sandwiches and hot food all day and night. The basement food halls of department stores, called depachika, are good for picnic supplies. The famous inner fish market moved from Tsukiji to Toyosu in 2018, but the Tsukiji Outer Market still has stalls for breakfast sushi and snacks. Tipping is not expected in Japan.

### Seasons, money and costs

Cherry blossoms usually peak in Tokyo in late March or early April, and this is one of the busiest and most expensive times to visit. Summer is hot and humid, and early autumn can bring typhoons. Cards are widely accepted, but small restaurants, shrines and markets may still want cash, and ATMs in 7-Eleven stores accept foreign cards. Trash cans are rare in public, so carry a small bag. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Tokyo at about 160 US dollars per person.

## Kyoto, Japan

### Getting there and around

Kyoto has no airport of its own. International travelers usually fly into Kansai International Airport (KIX) near Osaka and take the JR Haruka limited express, which reaches Kyoto Station in about 75 to 80 minutes. From Tokyo, the shinkansen bullet train takes about 2 hours 15 minutes. Inside the city, buses reach most temples but are often crowded; the two subway lines and the private Keihan and Hankyu railways are faster for some trips. Renting a bicycle works well because central Kyoto is flat. IC cards such as Suica and ICOCA are accepted on buses and trains.

### Temples, shrines and districts

Fushimi Inari Taisha, famous for thousands of red torii gates climbing the mountain, is open 24 hours and free; arriving early in the morning avoids the crowds. Kiyomizu-dera has a wooden stage with views over the city, and Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, is best on a sunny day. The Arashiyama bamboo grove is busiest from mid-morning. Gion is the traditional geisha district; some private lanes now ban photography, and visitors should never stop or touch geiko and maiko in the street.

### Food, seasons and costs

Kyoto is known for kaiseki, a multi-course seasonal meal, and for tofu dishes such as yudofu. Nishiki Market, a long covered street of food stalls, is good for tasting pickles, sweets and skewers, though eating while walking is discouraged there. The nearby town of Uji is famous for matcha green tea. Cherry blossoms in early April and autumn leaves from mid to late November are the most beautiful and most crowded seasons, so book hotels months ahead. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Kyoto at about 130 US dollars per person.

## Osaka, Japan

### Getting around

Osaka is served by Kansai International Airport (KIX), built on an island in Osaka Bay. The Nankai Rapi:t train reaches Namba in about 35 to 40 minutes, and the JR Haruka goes to Tennoji and on to Kyoto. Within the city, the Metro and JR Osaka Loop Line cover the main districts, and IC cards such as ICOCA or Suica work on both. Osaka is a convenient base for day trips: Kyoto is about 15 to 30 minutes away by train, Nara about 45 minutes, and Kobe about 25 minutes.

### Food and nightlife

Osaka calls itself the nation's kitchen, and its motto kuidaore roughly means eat until you drop. Dotonbori, along the canal with the famous running-man Glico sign, is the center of street food: takoyaki octopus balls, okonomiyaki savory pancakes and kushikatsu, deep-fried skewers. In kushikatsu shops the shared sauce pot follows a strict no double dipping rule. Kuromon Market is a covered market for seafood and snacks. Osaka is generally a little cheaper than Tokyo for food and hotels. Shinsekai, an older district near Tsutenkaku Tower, is the classic place for kushikatsu.

### Sights and day trips

Osaka Castle, rebuilt in the twentieth century, has a museum inside and a large park that is popular in cherry blossom season. Universal Studios Japan, with its Super Nintendo World area, is a major draw for families, and timed entry passes for the most popular areas can be needed on busy days. Nara, an easy day trip, has the huge bronze Buddha at Todai-ji and hundreds of free-roaming deer in Nara Park. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Osaka at about 120 US dollars per person.

## Lisbon, Portugal

### Getting around

Lisbon is built on steep hills, so comfortable shoes matter. The Metro's Red Line connects Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) to the city in about 20 minutes. Historic Tram 28 climbs through Graça, Alfama and Baixa, but it is often packed and known for pickpockets, so ride early in the morning. Funiculars and the Santa Justa lift help with the steepest climbs. A rechargeable Viva Viagem card works on the Metro, trams, buses and ferries. Trains from Rossio station reach Sintra in about 40 minutes, and trains from Cais do Sodré run along the coast to Cascais.

### Neighborhoods and sights

Alfama is the oldest district, a maze of lanes below São Jorge Castle, and the best place to hear fado, Portugal's melancholy traditional music, in small restaurants. Baixa is the flat, grid-planned downtown rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake, while Chiado and Bairro Alto are known for shopping by day and bars by night. In Belém, west of the center, the Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower are UNESCO World Heritage sites. Sintra, with its palaces and gardens in the hills, makes a popular day trip.

### Food, seasons and costs

The pastel de nata, a custard tart, is Lisbon's signature pastry, and Pastéis de Belém has made its version since 1837. Bacalhau, salted cod, appears on almost every menu, and grilled sardines take over the streets during the Santo António festivities in June. A bifana, a pork sandwich, is a cheap lunch. Lisbon is mild in winter and sunny from May to October, with July and August the hottest and busiest months. A tourist tax is charged per night. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Lisbon at about 95 US dollars per person.

## Porto, Portugal

### Getting around

Porto is compact but hilly. Metro line E, the violet line, connects Francisco Sá Carneiro Airport (OPO) to the city center in about 30 minutes, and riders pay with a rechargeable Andante card. The upper deck of the Dom Luís I bridge carries Metro trains and pedestrians across the Douro River to Vila Nova de Gaia, while the lower deck carries cars and walkers along the waterfront. The Guindais funicular saves the climb between the riverside and the upper town. Trains from São Bento and Campanhã stations run up the Douro Valley.

### Sights and wine

The Ribeira district along the river is the postcard view of Porto, with colorful houses facing the port wine cellars on the Gaia side, where most lodges offer tours and tastings. São Bento railway station is famous for its blue and white azulejo tile panels. Livraria Lello, a bookshop with an ornate staircase, requires a paid ticket that can be deducted from a book purchase. The Douro Valley, where port grapes are grown on terraced hillsides, can be visited on a day trip by train, river cruise or tour.

### Food and costs

The francesinha is Porto's signature dish: a sandwich layered with meats, covered in melted cheese and a beer-based sauce, often served with fries. Tripas à moda do Porto, a tripe and bean stew, explains why locals are nicknamed tripeiros. Porto is one of the most affordable cities in western Europe, and TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day there at about 85 US dollars per person, the lowest of all twelve destinations in the app. June 23 is the São João festival, Porto's biggest party.

## Reykjavik, Iceland

### Getting there and around

Keflavík International Airport (KEF) is about 50 kilometers from Reykjavik, and airport buses such as the Flybus take about 45 minutes to reach the city. Iceland has no passenger railway, so travel outside the capital is by rental car, tour bus or domestic flight. Reykjavik itself is small and walkable, with a city bus network called Strætó. Drivers should check road conditions and weather before every trip, because conditions change quickly; highland F-roads are open only in summer and only to four-wheel-drive vehicles.

### Sights and day trips

Hallgrímskirkja church has a tower with a view over the colorful roofs of the city, and the glass Harpa concert hall sits on the harbor. The Golden Circle, the most popular day trip, links Þingvellir National Park, the Geysir geothermal area where Strokkur erupts every few minutes, and Gullfoss waterfall. The Blue Lagoon geothermal spa is near the airport, so many travelers visit on the way in or out; entry must be booked in advance. Whale watching tours leave from the old harbor.

### Northern lights and midnight sun

The northern lights can only be seen when the sky is dark, so the season runs from roughly September to April; clear skies and solar activity both matter, and sightings are never guaranteed. Tours drive away from city lights on promising nights. In June the sun barely sets, and the midnight sun gives almost 24 hours of daylight, which is good for long road trips but means no aurora. Summer temperatures in Reykjavik usually stay between 10 and 15 degrees Celsius, so warm layers and a waterproof jacket are needed all year.

### Costs and practical tips

Iceland is one of the most expensive countries in Europe. Restaurant meals and especially alcohol cost much more than in most countries, so many travelers buy groceries at budget supermarkets such as Bónus. Tap water is excellent and free, and tipping is not expected. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, even for small amounts, and many fuel pumps need a card with a PIN. Swimming pools heated by geothermal water are cheap and found in every neighborhood. TakeOff's destination data puts a typical day in Reykjavik at about 210 US dollars per person, among the most expensive destinations in the app.
